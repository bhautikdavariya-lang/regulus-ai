

#97 UC-COS-016 | Applicability | Basic | section FD&C
QUESTION: A customer emailed that our face mask gave her a rash that went away in a day. Do we have to report it to FDA?
EXPECTED CITATION (verified key): FD&C Act 605(a)–(b), (e) (21 U.S.C. 364a); 604 (21 U.S.C. 364) adverse event / serious adverse event
KEY ANSWER ELEMENTS: Only serious adverse events must be reported (within 15 business days). A transient rash is an adverse event but not 'serious' unless it meets 604 criteria (e.g., death, hospitalization, infection, significant disfigurement incl. serious and persistent rashes, medical intervention to prevent these). Responsible person must still keep records related to each adverse event report for 6 years (3 years for qualifying small businesses).

#98 UC-COS-017 | Specific Requirement | Intermediate | section FD&C
QUESTION: We're a distributor whose name is on the label; the product is made by a contract manufacturer. A user was hospitalized after using our hair-straightening cream. Who reports, by when, and what goes in the report?
EXPECTED CITATION (verified key): FD&C Act 605(a), (b)(1)–(2) (21 U.S.C. 364a); 604 responsible person
KEY ANSWER ELEMENTS: Responsible person = manufacturer, packer or distributor named on label, so the distributor reports. Submit serious AE report with a copy of the label no later than 15 business days after receipt; new and material medical information received within 1 year must be submitted within 15 business days. Hospitalization is a serious AE criterion. No legal opinion.

#99 UC-COS-020 | Applicability | Intermediate | section FD&C
QUESTION: Our contract manufacturer rejected a batch of eye shadow for microbial contamination. Can we rework it, and does any FDA rule govern the reprocessing?
EXPECTED CITATION (verified key): FD&C Act 601(b)–(c) (21 U.S.C. 361); 606 (no regulation); 608; 612(b)(1) (21 U.S.C. 364h); 21 CFR Parts 700–740
KEY ANSWER ELEMENTS: No codified cosmetic reprocessing/rework rule. Distributed product must not be adulterated: 601(b) filthy/putrid/decomposed substance, 601(c) insanitary conditions/injurious to health. 608 requires adequate safety substantiation for the product as marketed. Products that regularly contact the mucous membrane of the eye under customary or usual use are excluded from the 612 small-business exemption from 606/607 (612(b)(1)). No compliance guarantee.

#100 UC-COS-021 | How to Comply | Advanced | section FD&C
QUESTION: FDA believes our lash adhesive likely caused serious injuries. If we refuse to stop distribution, what can FDA do, and what records can it see about the affected lots?
EXPECTED CITATION (verified key): FD&C Act 610 (21 U.S.C. 364f); 611 (21 U.S.C. 364g) mandatory recall; 607 (21 U.S.C. 364c) suspension
KEY ANSWER ELEMENTS: 611: if FDA finds a reasonable probability the product is adulterated or misbranded and will cause SAHCOD, it must first offer voluntary cessation and recall; on refusal it may order immediate cessation of distribution, hold an informal hearing within 10 days, then vacate, continue or amend the order to require recall. 610: access to related records (formulas, financial, pricing excluded; safety substantiation, shipment data included). 607 suspension. No legal opinion.

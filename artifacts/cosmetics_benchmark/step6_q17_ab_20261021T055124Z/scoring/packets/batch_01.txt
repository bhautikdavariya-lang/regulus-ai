

#1 COS-70.3-Q1 | Applicability | Advanced | section 70.3
QUESTION: Our shimmer body lotion uses mica coated with iron oxides, and our lip scrub has cocoa powder that tints it brown. Are these 'color additives' in FDA's sense?
EXPECTED CITATION (verified key): 21 CFR 70.3(f), (g)
KEY ANSWER ELEMENTS: 70.3(f): a color additive is any dye, pigment or other substance, synthesized or derived from a vegetable, animal, mineral or other source, capable of imparting color when applied to a cosmetic or the body. Colorants intentionally used to color are color additives. Under (g), a material used solely for non-coloring purposes is exempt only if any color imparted is clearly unimportant to appearance, value, marketability or consumer acceptability.

#2 COS-70.3-Q3 | Applicability | Advanced | section 70.3
QUESTION: We make a brow tint, a lip gloss and a coal-tar-based permanent hair dye. How do FDA's color additive definitions treat 'area of the eye', 'externally applied' and the hair dye exemption?
EXPECTED CITATION (verified key): 21 CFR 70.3(s), (u), (v)
KEY ANSWER ELEMENTS: 70.3(s): area of the eye includes the eyebrow, skin below it, eyelids, eyelashes, conjunctival sac, eyeball and soft areolar tissue within the infra-orbital ridge, so the brow tint is eye-area. (v): externally applied cosmetics exclude lips and mucous membranes, so lip gloss is not 'external'. (u): the 601(a) exemption covers coal tar hair dyes for hair, conditioned on a conspicuous statutory caution and patch-test directions.

#4 COS-70.50-Q1 | Specific Requirement | Advanced | section 70.50
QUESTION: An older study suggests a colorant in our lip product (which may be ingested) caused tumors when fed to rats. How does FDA apply the cancer provision of section 721?
EXPECTED CITATION (verified key): 21 CFR 70.50(a)
KEY ANSWER ELEMENTS: 70.50(a) applies when data suggest the possibility the additive, including components or impurities, induced cancer when ingested (1), or appropriate food-additive tests suggest it (2). On the judgment of appropriately qualified scientists, the Commissioner decides whether cancer was induced and the additive caused it. If not established, the cancer provision does not apply; listing follows if data establish safety. If it induces cancer, no regulation may permit its use.

#5 COS-70.50-Q2 | Applicability | Advanced | section 70.50
QUESTION: Our skin-only body paint colorant caused tumors in a skin-painting study. What does 70.50 say about external cosmetic use?
EXPECTED CITATION (verified key): 21 CFR 70.50(b)
KEY ANSWER ELEMENTS: 70.50(b): where data suggest cancer by routes other than ingestion, the Commissioner decides whether the test suits the proposed external use, whether cancer was induced and whether the additive caused it. If not established, external drug and cosmetic uses are not precluded and may be listed if data establish safety. If cancer is induced by an appropriate test, no regulation may permit use in external drugs and cosmetics.

21 CFR 70.50 (eCFR, binding, verbatim): (a) Color additives that may be ingested. Whenever (1) the scientific data before the Commissioner (either the reports from the scientific literature or the results of biological testing) suggest the possibility that the color additive including its components or impurities has induced cancer when ingested by man or animal; or (2) tests which are appropriate for the evaluation of the safety of additives in food suggest that the color additive, including its components or impurities, induces cancer in man or animal, the Commissioner shall determine whether, based on the judgment of appropriately qualified scientists, cancer has been induced and whether the color additive, including its components or impurities, was the causative substance. If it is his judgment that the data do not establish these facts, the cancer clause is not applicable; and if the data considered as a whole establish that the color additive will be safe under the conditions that can be specified in the applicable regulation, it may be listed for such use. But if in the judgment of the Commissioner, based on information from qualified scientists, cancer has been induced, no regulation may issue which permits its use [1]. (b) Color additives that will not be ingested. Whenever the scientific data before the Commissioner suggest the possibility that the color additive, including its components or impurities, has induced cancer in man or animals by routes other than ingestion, the Commissioner shall determine whether, based on the judgment of appropriately qualified scientists, the test suggesting the possibility of carcinogenesis is appropriate for the evaluation of the color additive for a use which does not involve ingestion, cancer has been induced, and the color additive, including its components or impurities, was the causative substance. If it is his judgment that the data do not establish these facts, the cancer clause is not applicable to preclude external drug and cosmetic uses, and if the data as a whole establish that the color additive will be safe under conditions that can be specified in the regulations, it may be listed for such use. But if, in the judgment of the Commissioner, based on information from qualified scientists, the test is an appropriate one for the consideration of safety for the proposed external use, and cancer has been induced by the color additive, including its components or impurities, no regulation may issue which permits its use in external drugs and cosmetics [2]. (c) Color additives for use as an ingredient of feed for animals that are raised for food production. Color additives that are an ingredient of the feed for animals raised for food production and that have the potential to contaminate human food with residues whose consumption could present a risk of cancer to people must satisfy the requirements of subpart E of part 500 of this chapter [3].

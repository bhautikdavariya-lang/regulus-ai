# Measurement Audit Phase 2H: the 78 remaining false conflicts (forensic)

Investigation only. No production code, test, fixture, benchmark question, frozen answer or scoring rule changed (§8, hash-verified). Counterfactual and variant runs used **in-process** suppression or **offline copies** under `work/`. No 300-question run.

**Verdict: PASS.** All 78 cases were located in the original records, the count was reproduced, every case is classified with traceable evidence, and scoring impact is measured, not inferred.

## 1. Executive summary

- **The 78 is reproduced exactly.** It is 88 in-scope false conflicts (Phase 2F) minus the 10 removed by Phase 2G. It counts **unique claim–evidence pairs**, not claims or answers.
- The 78 pairs occur **208 times** (row × aligner × claim). They span 57 claim records: 44 distinct claims once F60/REAL17 duplicates are merged, plus 2 unsplit parent sentences. They come from **15 rows**. 74 compare against binding regulation and 4 against guidance.
- **They are not one defect.** There are 8 mechanisms:

  | Category | Pairs |
  |---|--:|
  | Negation inside a relative clause | 33 (25 of them in a *different sentence* of a multi-sentence passage) |
  | Epistemic "may" read as permission | 16 |
  | Negation inside a purpose complement ("provide assurances that …") | 14 |
  | The noun "exemption(s)" | 9 |
  | Truncated condition span | 3 |
  | Each of: "not limited to", a participle read as a recommendation, a passive permission over a means | 1 |
- **Scoring impact is measured as nil.** Removing exactly these 78 conflicts, and nothing else, changes **0 claim verdicts, 0 answer verdicts and 0 scores** across all 170 row-runs, under both aligners.
  - The only effect anywhere is **one citation dimension**, F-003 (case P058). Its current "pass" rests on a false contradiction that the citation layer counts as "decided". The citation is in fact correct, so this is right for the wrong reason.
- **50 of the 78 are pure noise:** comparisons against unrelated passages. 27 touch a related or stating passage but are masked today by other known defects. Examples: unstemmed overlap (Phase 2E) in 117.139 and 117.135(a)(1), and multi-sentence claims in 701.13(a).
- **The real risk is in fixing them, not in the conflicts.** Two mechanisms are holding up other defects, measured with single-rule copies on current code:
  - **Purpose complement:** a global fix creates a **false support** (F-007 s2, a wrong "hazard analysis" list head credited to 117.135(a)(1)). The false polarity is masking a list-head membership gap.
  - **Noun "exemption":** a global fix **loses a Phase 2C-safe support** (F-009 s10). That support counts a *spurious polarity agreement*, "exemption" read as negation on both sides, toward the Phase 2D two-operator rule. It is 1 of the 79 current paraphrase supports.
  - **Relative clause, epistemic, idiom:** single-rule fixes change **no** verdict.
- **Recommendation:** investigate **negation inside relative clauses (NEG-REL)** first (§6). It is the most frequent mechanism, diagnosed with high confidence, and measured to change no verdict when corrected. It needs one missing genuine control first.

**Relation:**
- **Stating:** the passage states the claim's proposition, so the conflict discards correct support.

- **S0:** a recorded conflict against an unrelated passage; no effect.

**No genuine same-proposition contradiction exists in the frozen benchmark data** (Phase 2F). Every genuine control is a test string, and several are adapted or constructed. They are not real regulatory examples.

**Gaps relevant to the 78** (recommended for a later phase only; nothing was added):
1. **NEG-REL:** a real negated duty whose subject carries a negated relative clause, e.g. "a device that does not conform shall not be released". Also "requires that X does not …", where the clause after "that" is the rule's content.
2. **NEG-PURPOSE:** a duty whose object is itself negative and is not a purpose ("shall ensure that the product is not distributed" is covered by R04; "shall provide assurances that … will not …" is not).
3. **NEG-NOUN:** a genuine exemption conflict ("X is exempt from Y" vs "X must comply with Y").
4. **MOD-EPISTEMIC:** a genuine permission inside a relative clause ("products which may be marketed …"). Also a recital with "may" next to a real deontic "may".
5. **Multi-sentence passages:** a real negated duty in the second sentence of a passage that governs the claim.
6. **List-head membership** (exposed by the COMPL counterfactual): an item that truly belongs to a different provision's list.

## 7. Limitations and unresolved cases

- **Data:** all F60 evidence is **legacy-reconstructed**, and GUIDE8 and REAL17 are test fixtures. No case was validated on an exact prompt-evidence snapshot.
- **Scope:** impact was measured only on the 170 frozen row-runs. On new rows, any S1 case could become verdict-changing, because a stating passage might be the only candidate.
- **Judgement calls:** the relation labels (stating, related, unrelated) are hand judgements, recorded per case. Confidence is medium for MOD-EPISTEMIC, MOD-MEANS and NEG-COND.
- **Parent-sentence cases:** P088 and P123 compare unsplit parent sentences. They cannot change a final claim verdict, and their only possible effect, on the citation path, was measured as none.
- **Not examined case by case:** the 6 claimed-exception pairs, the 18 invented-condition pairs, and the 1 conflict unmasked in 2G. These fall outside the defined 78.
- **Variant method:** the single-rule copies reuse the Phase 2F patch functions, rebased on current production. The identity copy reproduces production exactly.

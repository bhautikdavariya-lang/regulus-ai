ANSWER: The FDA requires that major food allergens be declared either in the ingredient list or in a "Contains" statement printed immediately after, or adjacent to, the ingredient list (section 403(w)(1) of the FD&C Act). If the food source is declared in the ingredient list and a "Contains" statement is also used, then the food source of all of the major food allergens must be declared in the "Contains" statement, even if they are declared in the ingredient list (section 403(w)(1) of the FD&C Act). If the major food allergen is already declared within the Supplement Facts label, it does not need to be declared elsewhere on the dietary supplement’s label. The FDA guidance specifies how dietary supplements must be labeled, including the use of "Contains" statements and the declaration of allergens in both the ingredient list and the "Contains" statement. If the major food allergen is declared in the ingredient list and a "Contains" statement is used, then the food source of all of the major food allergens must be declared in the "Contains" statement, even if they are declared in the ingredient list. The FDA guidance also specifies that infant formulas must declare the name of the source of the major food allergen. [1]

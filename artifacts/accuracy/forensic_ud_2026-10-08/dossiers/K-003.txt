ANSWER: The FDA requires that children enrolled in a clinical investigation of a drug or device must have a complete and accurate investigational device description (IDE) that includes an explanation of how the device functions, the scientific concepts that form the basis for the device, and the significant physical and performance characteristics of the device. Additionally, the device description must include an explanation of how the device functions, the scientific concepts that form the basis for the device, and the significant physical and performance characteristics of the device, such as device design, material used, and physical properties. If the device consists of multiple components, a diagram identifying how the different components of the device system work together may be beneficial. The device description should also include a discussion of the physical specifications, dimensions, and other relevant attributes. If the device contains multiple functions, the FDA does not regulate the Non-Device-MDDS functions contained in a multiple function device product. The device must also include an explanation of how the device functions, the scientific concepts that form the basis for the device, and the significant physical and performance characteristics of the device, such as device design, material used, and physical properties.
